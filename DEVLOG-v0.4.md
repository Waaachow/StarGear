# StarGear — Episode 0: The Ghost Signal — v0.4

The biggest update yet. v0.3 made the sector feel alive; **v0.4 gives you a reason to fly
around it** — an economy, places to dock, work to take on, and a ship you rebuild yourself.

---

## 💰 New: Scrap, coin, and a hold that fills up

Every ship you destroy now leaves **scrap**, collected automatically on the kill. Scrap
isn't money — it's cargo. Your hold has a **hard capacity**, and once it's full, pickups
simply stop. That's the pressure: you have to go somewhere and sell.

**Coin** is the actual currency, and the only way to get it is to trade. Repairs and new
parts are paid in coin. A resource readout sits in the corner of the screen the whole time,
turning amber as the hold fills and red when it's full.

## 🛰️ New: Trade stations

Four manned stations are scattered across the sector — **Waypoint Hub**, **Veil Anchorage**,
**Quarry Station** and **Salvage Reach** — one near each region. They always show on the
Star Map and mini-map whether or not you've charted the space, so the loop is never
unfindable. Fly close and press **E** to dock.

Inside, tabs down the side let you:

- **Sell scrap** for coin.
- **Repair broken modules**.
- **Buy and sell ship parts** — you now *own* parts separately from having them installed.
- **Trade commodities** — four goods with **per-station prices**. Ore is cheap at the mining
  station and dear out at the nebula, so running a cargo of the right thing to the right
  place is real profit. Goods share the hold with scrap, which makes loading up a decision.
  Each row tells you where else you've seen the stuff sell, and for how much.
- **Take bounties and jobs** — see below.

Pull up alongside a station and it **asks whether you want to dock**, the same way the story
asks before it moves you on — no more flying past the thing you were heading for.

## 🎨 New: Every station is its own place

No two stations look alike, in space or inside. **Waypoint Hub** is a tidy brass ring station
with a lamplit counter and a window full of traffic. **Quarry Station** is an ore platform
with a crusher glowing hot underneath and the Broken Moon framed in its airlock. **Veil
Anchorage** is a patchwork hull grown onto an asteroid, nebula colour soaked into the plating
and a case of relics under the lamp. **Salvage Reach** is built inside the cut-open corpse of
a capital warship, tools racked along the bulkhead.

Docking somewhere is arriving at a place now, not opening the same menu in four locations.

## 👋 New: Somebody to talk to

Every station has a **keeper** standing behind the counter, and they're all different people:

- **Marla Quen** runs Waypoint Hub — brisk, warm, treats you like a regular from the first
  minute. The friendliest voice in the sector.
- **Oris Vale** deals relics at Veil Anchorage. Half appraiser, half mystic, and in no hurry
  to tell you what he knows.
- **Dova Krezh** runs the ore works at Quarry Station. Blunt, fair, and done talking.
- **Wick** runs the scrapyard at Salvage Reach, and will buy absolutely anything, at speed.

They **greet you when you dock and see you off when you leave** — and they're **fully
voiced**. The send-off plays with the trade menu cleared away, so the last thing you see is
the keeper in their own room rather than a list of prices.

## 📈 New: Stations have their own stock — and markets that move

Every station keeps a **finite inventory** of each commodity, and the price moves with what's
on the shelf. Buy a station out of ore and the last units cost noticeably more than the
first. Dump a full hold somewhere and you'll **flood the local market** — the price drops as
you sell, so a big load fetches less per unit than the sticker price.

Stations also **buy for less than they sell**, the way any trader does. Profit comes from
carrying goods somewhere they're worth more, never from standing at one counter — so a
route is a journey, not a button.

The upshot: a good trade route pays well the first time you run it, less the second, and
less again the third, until the shelves recover in their own time. Each commodity restocks
at its own pace, and the dear ones are the slowest — Nebula Relics are the richest run in
the sector *and* the one you can't repeat in a hurry. Trading is a genuine earner, not a
printing press.

## 🧭 New: The market remembers what you've seen

Knowing ore is cheap at the Quarry is only half a trade — the other half is knowing who
pays for it. **Dock somewhere and you remember its prices.** From then on, every market row
tells you the best price you've *seen* elsewhere, what you'd make a unit, how long ago you
saw it, and how far away it is:

> Buy Raw Ore ×10 · *running short · 160 in stock · cheap here*
> **▸ Veil Anchorage pays 14 each · +6 a unit · seen just now · 55 tiles NW**

Somewhere you've never docked simply says so — the sector's prices are something you go and
find out, and they can go stale while you're away. Your **cargo manifest** does the same
job from the other end: every good in the hold, and the best buyer you know of for it.

## 💷 New: You can't be made to sell at a loss

Sell a commodity and the station takes **as much as it can pay you a profit on**, and stops
there. If you've already crashed the local price, the rest stays in your hold to carry
somewhere better — the row tells you exactly how many are worth selling and how many aren't.

If you'd rather have the space than the margin, **Dump all** is right underneath, with the
real number on it. Clearing the hold at a loss is always available; it's just never
something the game does to you behind your back.

You also now start with **250 coin**. The market was previously out of reach until you'd
fought something and sold the salvage, which meant a lot of first sessions never saw the
trade loop at all. It's enough for a couple of lots — the rest you earn.

## 🔧 New: Ship Config — build your own ship

The Ship Config screen is now a **module fit-grid** laid over the ship's blueprint. Every
part is a shaped piece you rotate and place, and **it only works if it's connected back to
the Reactor Core**.

The catch: **those modules *are* your crew's combat abilities.** Rex's Missile Pod is a
part on the grid. Pull it out and Rex loses Missile. Pull both his weapon modules and Rex
is **offline** in battle. Kael's Sensor Suite powers his Scan *and* widens your exploration
sweep. Astra's drive modules are her Maneuver and Space Hop.

Fitting the ship is now a real trade-off against cargo space, armour and firepower.

## 💀 New: Bounty contracts — three boss fights

Accept a contract at a station's bounty board and its target is **marked straight away** —
in the sector and on the Star Map. Take all three if you like; every accepted mark is live
at once, and you clear them in whatever order your route suggests. Fly to a mark and the
fight begins. Each is a **boss with its own tactics**, not a tougher regular ship:

- **Captain Malachar Drax, "The Red Reaver"** — a decorated fleet commander of the Border
  Conflicts whose government decommissioned his flagship, forgot his soldiers and quietly
  erased his name. He stole an experimental warship out of a scrapyard, rebuilt it as the
  **Blood Crown**, and went to war on everyone who profited from men like him. He fights the
  way he always won: the Blood Crown is **untouchable while any of his escort wing lives**,
  and he whistles up a fresh pair of **Hounds** every few turns — they die for him and he
  lets them. Clear the wing and burn him down inside the gap.

  Drax gets a **full encounter**: his own art as the fight opens, and **voiced dialogue** that
  answers you as the fight turns — when you first meet, when you've taken half his hull, when
  he's nearly done, and at the end. He never shouts.
- **Sable Renn, "The Afterimage"** — a courier who fitted a salvaged prototype blink drive to
  her ship, the **Half-Light**, and can't stop using it: every jump phases the pilot along with
  the hull, and she comes back a little less complete each time. She fights by never being
  where you shot, telegraphing her jump and shedding a **live mine** wherever she leaves. She
  rarely kills anyone — she strands them, which the shipping lanes have decided is worse.
  Read the marker and be waiting where she lands.
- **Harkin Dross, "The Tollman"** — twenty years a haulage foreman, until the mining combine
  cut the crews loose and kept the ore. He kept the hauler. The **Tithe** sits broadside across
  the southern drift behind **three blast plates that rotate and rebuild**, and he charges a
  fee to pass. Pay and he lets you through, scrupulously. Refuse and he comes through you,
  down a ram lane telegraphed straight along your row. He does not consider himself a pirate.

All three are **fully voiced**, and each answers you as the fight turns — when you meet, at
half hull, near the end, and after.

## 📋 New: Side missions

Multi-step jobs, each **posted at one station only**, so you have to go looking for work
rather than finding the same list everywhere. Haul medical supplies across the sector,
investigate an echo out in the nebula, bank a load of salvage. **Accepting a job starts it**,
and steps tick off as you go — collection objectives show a running count, so "Bank 40
scrap" reads **17 / 40** in the Missions menu instead of leaving you guessing.

**Your mission log only shows what you've actually taken** — station boards are where you
find work, so the map stays quiet until you commit.

## 🔍 New: Exploration scan

Press **F** to sweep the space around you for salvage and trade goods. Range depends on your
ship: **6 tiles bare, +5 with a powered Sensor Suite**, +2 with a bigger hold — and with no
working Sensor Suite it won't sweep at all. Sites are fixed to the map and **consumed once
found**, so nowhere can be farmed.

Scanning pays in **cargo, not cash** — you still have to get it to a buyer. Salvage turns up
occasionally; the great majority of what you find is trade goods.

And a hull only has to be scanned **once**: identify a ship type in battle and every ship of
that type reads as scanned from then on, wherever you meet it. Kael's Scan is for the ones
you haven't seen before.

## 🗣️ New: The crew talk to each other — out loud

The long quiet stretches between objectives are gone. The crew now comment on where you are,
what you've run into, how the fight went, and when the hold is full — **34 lines, all fully
voiced**, with subtitles in each character's colour.

And now they talk **in battle too.** A crew member will *occasionally* throw out a line when
they act — Rex over his lasers, Tessa mid-repair, Kael raising a shield — and react to the
moments that matter: a ship going down, a crewmate knocked out, a hit that really lands. It's
tuned to be flavour, not chatter: roughly one action in three speaks up, and never two at once.
Scripted boss dialogue always wins the bar. **48 lines, all fully voiced.**

## 🔊 New: Sound

The game had no sound effects at all. It now has a full layer: laser and missile fire, hits,
shields absorbing a hit, **near-misses**, explosions, the scan ping, salvage pickup, sales,
and docking clamps — plus a distinct **dockside music track** that fades in when you arrive
at a station.

## 💾 New: Save and load — including save files you can keep

**Three save slots**, holding *everything*: your position, economy and cargo, ship loadout,
owned and broken modules, codex, achievements, contracts, station markets, your charted map —
**and where you are in the story**. Save mid-episode and loading puts you back in the episode,
not out in free roam.

Save from the ship menu, or **load straight from the title screen** — LOAD GAME is always
there, listing each slot with its date, whether it's a story or free-roam run, coin, scrap
and where you were standing.

You can also **download a save to a file** and load one back in, from either the title screen
or the in-game save menu. Back a run up, move it to another machine, or keep a copy before
you try something reckless.

## 🏆 New: Achievements

Four for Episode 0 — finish the story, clear the bounty board, complete every side job, and
scan every ship in the database. Progress bars with a banner when one unlocks, on the
**AWARDS** tab of the Missions screen.

## 💥 Losing a fight is no longer the end — but it costs you

Defeat used to be a game over. Now **one or two of your modules break** — they still take up
their space on the grid, but their effect is dead until you pay to repair them at a station.
You limp away and carry on. A broken part is a bill, not a reset.

Whoever broke you off also **helps themselves to the hold on the way out**: you lose
**30–50% of your cargo**, scrap and trade goods alike. Your **coin is safe** — that's banked,
not stowed — so a bad fight costs you the run you were carrying, not everything you own.
Limping home half-empty is survivable. It's just expensive.

And **beating the story doesn't end the run** — the ending screen has a **RETURN TO THE
SECTOR** option that drops you back into free roam with everything you earned, for the
bounties and jobs you left behind.

## 🗺️ Getting around

- **Mini-map** in the corner — nearby charted space, hostiles, and your current objective.
- **Star Map legend** explaining every marker and zone colour.
- **Per-hull attack visuals** — each enemy hull now has a signature shot you can recognise
  without reading the log.

## 🎬 New: The Journey Begins

Episode 0 now opens on a **twelve-shot cinematic** between the title screen and the bridge —
Captain Voss narrating over the sector waking up around you, each line landing on its own
image. The text and the pictures are cut to the recording itself rather than to a timer, so
they stay in step.

## 🎚️ New: Sound levels, and a cargo manifest

**Options now has a mixer** — **Master, Voice, Music and Effects**, adjusted with the arrow
keys or by clicking the bar, and remembered between sessions. Turn the music down without
losing the crew, or the voices down without going silent.

There's also a **cargo manifest**, on the CARGO tab of the Ship screen: your hold itemised,
with a capacity bar, **the best buyer you know of for each commodity**, and your coin listed
separately — because coin isn't cargo and can't be taken off you.

## 📖 New: A tidier ship menu

The menu had grown to nine entries, so related screens now sit together. It reads
**Star Map · Ship · Crew · Missions · Ship Database · Save / Load · Field Manual · Options**.

**Ship** holds both halves of your ship — **CONFIGURATION** (the module grid) and **CARGO**
(the hold), with the hold's fill shown right on the tab. **Missions** gained an **AWARDS**
tab for achievements. Left and right move between tabs anywhere they appear.

## 📕 New: The Field Manual, and tips that arrive when they're relevant

v0.4 added an economy, stations, trade, module fitting, side jobs and bounties — and the
game was still explaining itself with the same two pop-ups it had when it was flying and
shooting. Both halves of that are fixed.

**The Field Manual** is a new menu entry: nine chapters covering everything the ship can
do — flight, scanning and the star map, docking and trade, cargo, modules and power, crew
and abilities, combat, missions and bounties, and saving. It's there from the first minute
and you can go back to it whenever, rather than having to catch an explanation the one
time it appears.

**First-time tips** now cover every system instead of two. The first time you scan, dock,
fill the hold, open the module grid, look at the mission board, take aim in a fight, run a
turn dry or lose a battle, a short pop-up explains what just happened and why it matters.
Each one shows once. If you'd rather work it out yourself, **Options → Tutorial Tips**
turns the lot off — the manual stays either way.

## ⇄ Clearer: every crew member has two abilities

This kept catching people out, so it's worth saying plainly: **all six crew carry two
abilities and you choose which one they take into a fight.** Rex has lasers or a missile.
Astra can fly or jump. Kael can scan or shield. A fight that seems impossible with one
loadout is often straightforward with another.

Switching from **Menu → Crew** between fights is **free**. Switching mid-battle still works
— it's a real option when a fight turns — but it costs that crew member's action for the
turn.

In battle, **⇄ Switch Action** now sits directly under the ability it swaps, instead of
below the two greyed "locked slot" rows where it read like more locked content. The tips
and the manual both spell the rule out, and the manual lists all six pairs.

## 🧭 Quality of life & fixes

- The **title screen fills the screen** at any aspect ratio, and its background sits still.
- **Scrap and coin stay visible** when you open the ship menu — and stay *out* of the way
  during a fight, where the combat HUD needs the room.
- **The sector opens empty.** No station sits on your doorstep at the start any more; the
  nearest one is a short flight out, so the first thing you do is look around.
- Arriving beside a station **asks once** whether to dock, instead of a permanent prompt
  sitting on screen. Changing your mind still just means pressing **E**.
- **UNDOCK appears once**, as a back button, rather than twice.
- The station screen **fits its content** instead of drawing a fixed slab over the artwork,
  so you can actually see the room you're standing in.
- New **scrap and coin icons** in the resource readout.
- **The Ship Database is ships.** Bounty hulls have their own cards now — the **Blood Crown**
  and the **Hound** wing that dies for it, the **Half-Light**, the **Tithe** — each with full
  stats, a tactics briefing, who flies it and what the contract pays. Eleven entries, and
  *Know Your Enemy* covers all of them.
- **Kael's Scan makes a sound** in battle.
- The **Star Map legend** no longer cuts off its last line.
- Fixed a line of Kael's dialogue that played in the wrong voice.
- The resource readout is **two lines, always** — **SCRAP** and **COIN**, the two numbers you
  actually act on. Trade goods no longer add a third line; they still show as the grey part of
  the hold bar, and the itemised manifest is a tab away on **Ship → CARGO**.
- Cargo modules say what they actually do — *"Capacity to hold 30 more scrap."*
- Episode 0 now **opens on the bridge** for a beat before anyone speaks.
- The crew sheet's **⇄ SWAP** button carries its own *1/2* counter, instead of printing it
  over the ability description.
- The top bar lists the keys it had been leaving out — **F** to scan and **E** to dock.

Thanks for flying with the StarGear — more to come. 🌌
