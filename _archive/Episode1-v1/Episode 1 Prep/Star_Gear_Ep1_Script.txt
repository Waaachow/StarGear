END OF PART 3
What this accomplishes

Instead of Voss confessing because Kael pressures him, the story naturally builds to that moment:

Astra begins to question herself privately.
The crew gathers fragments of the truth.
Rowan shows that not everyone who fears Astra is cruel—some are carrying genuine trauma.
Dax confirms Astra is significant without revealing everything.
The recovered GearWorks archive becomes the catalyst that Voss can no longer ignore.

It also gives Astra a quiet emotional journey before the revelation. By the time Voss says, "It's time I told you everything," the player isn't just curious about Astra's origins—they're invested in what that truth will mean for her. That's a much stronger foundation for Part 4, which should be the emotional heart of the episode.

Why I Changed This

I think this version strengthens every character:

Voss doesn't hide the truth out of shame or manipulation. He genuinely believes Astra deserved the chance to define herself before her past defined her.
Kael doesn't suddenly forgive Voss. He still thinks the captain made the wrong call—but he respects the reasoning.
Rex, Tessa, and Selyra respond exactly as you'd expect from a found family. Their first instinct isn't to question Astra; it's to reassure her that she's already proven who she is.
Astra ends this act no longer asking, "Who am I?" Instead, she's ready to answer that question herself.

Ending with Captain Raze's first appearance also gives the second half of the episode a clear shift in momentum. The mystery is over. Now the conflict is personal: Raze isn't trying to destroy the StarGear—he's trying to reclaim someone the crew already considers family. That raises the stakes naturally heading into the final act.

Why this works

I think this is a stronger climax because Raze never becomes a cartoon villain. He genuinely believes Astra belongs with him because she represents the pinnacle of his life's work. He offers her knowledge, answers, and even her lost memories—not out of kindness, but because he values her as an extraordinary creation.

The emotional peak isn't the boss battle itself. It's Astra's quiet line:

"I already am."

She doesn't reject her past. She simply chooses the family she has now over the purpose she was manufactured for. That decision belongs entirely to her, making the battle that follows feel like the consequence of her choice rather than something the crew chooses on her behalf.

Why I'd end it this way

There's one major addition here that I think strengthens not just Episode 1, but the entire series: A-17 survives.

Not as a new crew member—not yet—but as living proof of the episode's central theme. Astra's defining act isn't defeating her mirror image; it's extending the same compassion Voss once extended to her. Voss trusted Astra to make the choice, and Astra chose mercy.

That closes the thematic circle beautifully:

Voss rescued Astra.
Astra rescues A-17.
A-17 becomes a seed for a future story, not because of a cliffhanger, but because she now has the opportunity Astra was once given: the chance to discover who she wants to be.

It also gives the season a subtle emotional thread. Every time the crew passes the medical bay in later episodes, players will remember that the StarGear isn't just a ship that wins battles—it's a place where people, even those built for war, are given the chance to become something more.
